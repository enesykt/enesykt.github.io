Search for Quasi-Periodic Oscillations in Gamma-Ray Bursts

Sep. 2026 – Present | Supervisor: Prof. Ersin Göğüş (Sabancı University)

I analyze Fermi/GBM light curves across different time intervals and energy bands to search for candidate quasi-periodic oscillations (QPOs) in gamma-ray bursts.

  • Modeling power spectra using power-law continua and Lorentzian profiles with Python and Stingray.
  • Using Gaussian Process modeling to characterize stochastic background variability and test whether candidate signals remain significant after accounting for analysis choices and multiple trials.
  • Checking the energy dependence of candidate features and comparing findings with published studies.

Quasi-Periodic Oscillation Analysis of V1405 Cas Using TESS Photometry

Oct. 2025 – Present | Supervisor: Prof. Ersin Göğüş (Sabancı University)

I study how oscillatory signals evolve in V1405 Cas (a classical nova and cataclysmic variable) as the system relaxes following an outburst.

  • Extended previous studies by identifying and adding two new post-outburst TESS sectors, increasing the time baseline for tracking variability.
  • Comparing pre-outburst and post-outburst power spectra sector by sector, fitting Gaussian and Lorentzian models to candidate QPO features.
  • Estimating detection significance and upper limits during phases where signals are weak, separating real accretion-driven variability from instrumental or pipeline artifacts.

TESS Photometry Cataclysmic Variables Power Spectral Analysis

Exoplanet Light Curve Formation and Calibration with TESS Photometry

Jan. 2024 – Present | Supervisor: Asst. Prof. Tansu Daylan (Washington University in St. Louis / @AstroMusers)

A long-term project focused on catalog-scale transit analysis of confirmed and candidate exoplanet targets observed by TESS.

  • Built reproducible Python pipelines to clean, detrend, normalize, and phase-fold TESS-SPOC and QLP light curves.
  • Applied Box Least Squares (BLS) periodograms to recover orbital periods, transit durations, and transit depths.
  • Systematically compared recovered parameters against ExoFOP-TESS and literature catalogs, diagnosing discrepancies caused by detrending artifacts, period aliases, or depth dilution.
  • Currently calculating expected timing precision limits from finite light-curve baselines as part of a manuscript in preparation.

Condensed Matter, Quantum Systems & Field Theory

Connectivity Conditions for Nagaoka Ferromagnetism in Honeycomb Lattices (Senior Thesis)

Dec. 2025 – May 2026 | Supervisors: Prof. Alev Devrim Güçlü (İzmir Institute of Technology) & Asst. Prof. Emir Haliki (Ege University)

For my undergraduate thesis, I investigated when and how finite honeycomb lattice structures satisfy the connectivity requirements for Nagaoka ferromagnetism.

  • Built triangular and hexagonal honeycomb clusters with varying boundary conditions and zigzag/armchair edges.
  • Modeled the one-hole Hubbard limit by mapping hole motion and spin configurations onto a connected state graph.
  • Used breadth-first search (BFS) algorithms to test reachability across many-body states under nearest-neighbor hopping and analyze finite-size effects.

Graphene Quantum Dots – Tight-Binding Modeling (Research Internship)

Aug. 2025 – Sep. 2025 | Supervisor: Prof. Alev Devrim Güçlü (İzmir Institute of Technology)

During my summer internship at IZTECH, I built tight-binding models from scratch to study the electronic properties of finite graphene structures.

  • Started from a six-site benzene-like ring and scaled up to hexagonal and triangular graphene quantum dots with zigzag and armchair edges.
  • Computed energy spectra, density of states (DOS), and site-resolved probability densities.
  • Extended the model to include next-nearest-neighbor hopping to study symmetry breaking, mid-gap states, and edge localization.
